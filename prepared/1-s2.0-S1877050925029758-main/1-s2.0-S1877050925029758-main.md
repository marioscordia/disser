## **Abstract** 

Effective and precise passage retrieval is crucial in contemporary information retrieval systems, particularly in light of the exponential expansion of digital document collections. Conventional approaches relying on lexical matching frequently struggle to capture semantic subtleties, often overlooking passages that are semantically pertinent but lexically divergent from user queries. Recent advancements in transformer-based models, such as BERT and its variants, have substantially enhanced retrieval performance by facilitating a deeper semantic comprehension. Nonetheless, the computational demands of these models present scalability challenges when applied to large datasets. 

In this work, we propose an innovative passage retrieval framework that seamlessly integrates lexical-semantic relevance with contextual information, markedly boosting retrieval precision and coherence. Our methodology unfolds in three stages: first, a Cross-Encoder BERT model assesses semantic relevance by analyzing fine-grained interactions between queries and passages. Second, a Bi-Encoder architecture generates initial embeddings, which are subsequently refined using a Graph Attention Network (GAT) to incorporate structural and contextual relationships among passages. Third, a dynamic reranking mechanism improves ranking by merging semantic relevance scores with contextual similarity evaluations, ensuring that retrieved passages are both contextually coherent and semantically precise. 

Evaluations conducted on benchmark datasets against state-of-the-art baselines reveal that our hybrid approach enhances retrieval accuracy and coherence. Furthermore, by integrating graph-based embedding refinements, our model mitigates the scalability constraints typically associated with Cross-Encoder architectures.

## **2. Related Works** 

Passage retrieval and ranking are essential components in the fields of information retrieval and natural language processing, playing a pivotal role in shaping user experiences. Over time, research has progressed from simple lexical matching techniques to more sophisticated models that integrate semantic comprehension and structural context. This section provides a concise overview of transformer-based and graph neural network-based methods, highlighting their advantages and drawbacks to frame the context for our proposed hybrid approach. 

## _2.1. Transformer-based Models_ 

Effective passage retrieval and ranking are fundamental tasks in information retrieval and natural language processing research. Traditional ranking methods primarily rely on lexical matching algorithms, such as BM25 [12], which use term frequency and inverse document frequency to rank passages. While efficient, these lexical approaches often struggle to handle semantic nuances, especially paraphrasing and semantic similarity [3]. 

To overcome these limitations, recent advancements in deep learning have introduced transformer-based architectures, like BERT [2], which have enhanced semantic understanding in retrieval tasks. Ouni et al. [8] proposed a classification approach combining BERT with topic-based features and convolutional neural networks (CNN), illus-

On the other hand, Bi-Encoder models, such as Sentence-BERT (SBERT) [11], alleviate this issue by generating independent dense embeddings for queries and passages, facilitating efficient similarity computation. 

BERT-based CNNs have also been applied to extract semantic-stylistic features for classification and scaled to sentiment analysis using DistilBERT within a distributed framework [9, 4]. 

## _2.2. Graph Neural Network-based Models_ 

Although transformer-based models excel in semantic understanding, they typically overlook contextual and structural relationships within documents. The emergence of Graph neural network (GNN) approaches, particularly Graph Attention Networks (GATs) [13], have gained popularity for incorporating structural context into retrieval tasks. These models capture relational structures among passages, significantly enhancing retrieval accuracy [16]. For example, Graph Retrieval [15] explicitly uses graph neural networks to propagate contextual information, effectively modeling intricate structural relationships across documents. The primary advantage of GNN models stems in their ability to incorporate document-level context, which improves coherence and accuracy. However, their reliance on structural information may lead to less sensitivity to direct semantic interactions between query and passage, potentially compromising immediate semantic relevance. 

Hybrid models combining transformer-based semantic embeddings and GNN-enhanced contextual information have emerged as promising solutions [7]. Methods such as Graph-Enhanced Transformer (GET) [5] and Contextualized Embeddings with Graph Attention [14] demonstrate how integrating semantic and structural contexts through graph attention mechanisms significantly refines retrieval outcomes. 

These studies demonstrate the flexibility of transformer-based models and the added value of integrating structural context through GNNs, confirming their complementary roles in enhancing retrieval effectiveness across diverse scenarios. 

## **3. Proposed ReGAT-BERT Details** 

Our proposed method, ReGAT-BERT, overcomes the limitations of transformer-based and GAT-based models by seamlessly combining the semantic precision of transformer architectures with the contextual coherence of GAT. This integration ensures a balanced approach, capturing both immediate semantic relevance and structural contextual relationships, thereby significantly improving retrieval performance. ReGAT-BERT is specifically designed to efficiently extract and prioritize the most relevant passages from large document collections, greatly enhancing the ability to address user queries. Our main objective is to advance passage ranking by using both lexical-semantic relevance and contextual relationships, enabling a deeper and more nuanced understanding of user queries and document content.

## _3.2. Lexical-Semantic Relevance_ 

The main goal of this phase is to accurately assess passage relevance by modeling fine-grained semantic interactions between the query and candidate passages. In our approach, we use a Cross-Encoder based on BERT to refine the evaluation of passage relevance in response to a given query. Unlike Bi-Encoder models, which generate independent representations for queries and passages, the Cross-Encoder processes these elements jointly, enabling a more precise modeling of semantic relationships. 

This phase is designed to compute the semantic relevance score, which quantifies the likelihood that a passage adequately answers the query. The model takes as input a query-passage pair, encoding it through BERT’s transformation layers to capture contextual relationships between terms. However, a major challenge of the Cross-Encoder lies in its computational cost, as it requires evaluating each query-passage pair individually. 

To alleviate this limitation, we apply a filtering mechanism that limits Cross-Encoder evaluation to a smaller group of passages that are more likely to be relevant. This filtering relies on a relevance propagation strategy that estimates the potential importance of passages based on the contextual signals of their parent sections or document structure. The idea is to propagate relevance scores from higher-level nodes (such as section titles or document headings) to child passages, using lexical or concept-based similarity. This significantly reduces the number of passages processed, improving computational efficiency while maintaining retrieval effectiveness.

This approach ensures that passage ranking is informed not only by direct semantic relevance but also by structural and relational context within the document collection.

## _3.3.2. Contextual Embedding Enhancement_

## **4. Experimental study and results analysis** 

This section presents the evaluation of our ReGAT-BERT model, comparing it with both internal baselines and state-of-the-art retrieval models. The evaluation is conducted on the MS MARCO Passage Ranking dataset using MRR@10 and NDCG@10 as the primary evaluation metrics. 

## _4.1. Experimental design_ 

To evaluate the effectiveness of ReGAT-BERT, we conducted experiments on the MS MARCO Passage Ranking dataset, a large-scale benchmark commonly used for passage retrieval tasks. This dataset consists of real-world user queries paired with relevant and non-relevant passages, making it suitable for assessing retrieval performance in a realistic setting. 

## _4.1.1. Dataset_ 

We evaluate our proposed method using the MS MARCO (Microsoft MAchine Reading COmprehension) dataset, a large-scale benchmark specifically designed for assessing passage retrieval and reranking methods in information retrieval. The dataset comprises 8.8 million passages extracted from web documents and approximately 1 million training queries. Additionally, it includes a development set for evaluation, ensuring that each query is associated with at least one relevant passage.

## **5. Limitations of the proposed approach** 

Our approach which is based on the integration of transformer models and GATs for dynamic reranking tasks has shown promising results in capturing both sequential dependencies and structural relationships within data. However, several limitations hinder their full potential. First, the computational complexity of combining these architectures often leads to scalability issues, particularly when dealing with large-scale datasets. Second, the dynamic nature of reranking tasks requires real-time adaptability, which is challenging due to the static nature of pre-trained transformer models and the difficulty in updating graph structures. Third, the interpretability of the integrated model remains limited, making it difficult to understand how the combination of attention mechanisms influences reranking decisions. Additionally, the reliance on high-quality graph construction for GATs can introduce biases or errors if the graph representation is suboptimal. Finally, the integration often requires extensive hyperparameter tuning and domain-

## **6. Conclusion and prospects** 

In this study, we introduced a novel passage reranking method designed to enhance information retrieval performance by seamlessly integrating lexical-semantic relevance with contextual information. By using transformer models, specifically Cross-Encoder BERT and Bi-Encoder architectures enhanced through Graph Attention Networks (GAT), our approach effectively addresses limitations associated with traditional lexical matching and purely semantic-based retrieval systems. Our advanced experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed hybrid approach substantially improves retrieval accuracy, semantic precision, and contextual coherence compared to existing state-of-the-art methodologies. Moreover, by dynamically reranking passages based on combined semantic and contextual scores, our framework achieves superior performance while efficiently managing computational resources, overcoming typical scalability challenges. This research not only advances the theoretical understanding of effective passage retrieval but also offers practical solutions that can be readily adopted in real-world applications involving complex textual data processing. 

In future work, we aim to explore two new directions. The first involves conducting a more in-depth study using data collections and analyses to address the challenge of dynamic reranking. Through this effort, we hope to validate and enhance the performance of our model. The second direction focuses on using machine learning techniques for dynamic reranking, which has the potential to substantially improve the effectiveness and outcomes of our approach.
