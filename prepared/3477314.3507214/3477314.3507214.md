## **ABSTRACT** 

Understanding[1] and predicting user click behavior on a web search engine results page is critical for online advertising and recommendation engines. The click prediction results can be further used to estimate the relevance of the search engine results. Sequence-to-sequence models are effective for predicting user click behavior since they consider contextual information in the input sequence, and transformers are the recent state of the art in sequence learning. A novel transformer model is proposed in this paper for click prediction and relevance estimation that learns additionally from the vertical information, apart from the query and search engine results that are the inputs for the traditional click models. Vertical information is the representation style of a document in the search engine results page. The proposed vertical-aware transformer click model (v-TCM) takes into account both the position bias and the vertical bias of the search engine results. From our experiments, it is proved that v-TCM outperforms the existing click models for the click prediction and relevance estimation tasks.

Our contribution is new from the perspective that we incorporate the vertical information type of each document in SERP (vertical bias) as an additional input to the encoder of a multi-head self-attention based transformer, apart from the query and the ranked search engine results (position bias) which is the conventional input of click prediction models. The main contributions of our paper are summarized as follows. 

- A novel vertical-aware transformer click model called v- TCM is proposed which takes the vertical bias into consideration. The new model is able to incorporate both the position and vertical biases into a calculable framework. 

- Transformers with multi-head self-attention constitute the state of the art in sequence learning, and this model is used for learning the position and vertical biases from user logs. 

- The model achieves significantly better performance in both click prediction and relevance estimation as compared to existing click models.

## **2 VERTICAL-AWARE TRANSFORMER CLICK MODEL** 

In this section, we describe the architecture of the proposed vertical-aware Transformer Click Model. Click modeling can be interpreted as a sequence modeling task. The query and the search engine results define the input sequence, and the prediction of whether the user will click on a search result or not is recorded for the 10 results displayed on the search page, and this defines the output sequence. Recurrent neural networks, specifically LSTMs, have been used for sequence-to-sequence modeling and prediction [12, 14]. Neural networks are found to capture context well using attention mechanism [15], and that improves the click prediction and relevance estimation results, as proved by researchers in [16]. However, since its inception in 2017 [11], transformers have overtaken LSTMs in most of the sequence modeling tasks. The primary advantage is the parallel computing and multi-head selfattention incorporated in the transformer architecture. In our work we propose to use the transformer for click prediction. We feed the query, position bias (ranked search engine results) and vertical bias as input to the transformer encoder, along with the target clicks. Fig. 2 shows the architecture of the proposed verticalaware transformer click model that we call as v-TCM.

The transformer PyTorch embedding layer is used to embed all the input features into distributed fixed-size 192-dimensional vectors. The input features are query _q_ , search engine result _dt_ , and the vertical type _vt_ of each result, where _t_ =1,2,…,10. We concatenate all the input embeddings and that is given as input to the transformer encoder, along with the embedded target click sequence, as shown in Fig. 2. Our multi-head transformer architecture used four heads whose outcomes are concatenated as shown in Fig. 2. To avoid overfitting, we make use of dropout and Leaky ReLU in both self-attention and feed forward network sublayers. We then use three fully connected layers to learn the interaction among the dense features. Since the problem is related to whether the user will click on a search result or not, therefore it is a binary classification problem, and thus we use the sigmoid function in the output unit. To train the model, the loss function used is the cross-entropy loss function. 

## **3 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND RESULTS** 

Through our experiments, we aim to answer the following research question: - does the transformer-based click model perform better in terms of click prediction and relevance estimation, and how incorporating the verticals into the click models affects the overall performance. In this section, we first outline the experimental setup. The results obtained are then analyzed with respect to the click prediction accuracy and relevance estimation. 

## **3.1 Dataset** 

We conducted our experiments on an open log-based session dataset named TianGong-ST [18]. The details are shown in Table 1. The SERP data is refined from the 18-day user log recorded by search engine Sogou.com. The dataset is split at the source into train, test and validation sets. The model is trained on the training set. The hyperparameters were set based on the model’s performance on the validation set. Finally for calculating the performance and evaluation metrics, the testing set was used. As noted from Table 1, there are 4491 unique verticals in this dataset that are assigned unique IDs.

## **3.2 Performance metrics** 

We evaluate the performance of v-TCM for click prediction and relevance estimation. For evaluating the click prediction results, 

we compute the perplexity (PPL) on the test set. The definition of click perplexity (PPL) at the rank r is given by

where, PPL(M) denotes the total perplexity of the model M. PM(Cr=cr[(s)] ) denotes the probability of click event cr[(s)] having rank r in a query session s. Here, S is the total number of sessions. The final perplexity of the model M is calculated by averaging the perplexities over all positions or ranks. Lower the value of perplexity, the higher will be the quality of the model. For relevance estimation, we use the relevance scores estimated by the click models to re-rank the document list, and calculate the normalized discounted cumulative gain (NDCG) according to the human annotation provided in the TianGong-ST dataset. A higher value of NDCG denotes better relevance estimation by the model. 

## **3.3 Baseline models** 

We compare the performance of our transformer click prediction model v-TCM with state-of-the-art click models like UBM, DCM, DBN, NCM and CSM. While UBM, DCM, DBN are probabilistic graphical models, NCM and CSM are neural models based on LSTM. We implemented these models in accordance with the guidelines in their original papers [1-5]. We have also included the transformer model (without vertical information) proposed recently for Alibaba recommendation machines [17]. 

## **3.4 Hyperparameter settings** 

For training the proposed models, we use a batch size of 64 and the Adam optimizer. Both the hidden layer size and the embedding dimension is fixed to 64. The initial learning rate and dropout rate is set to 0.001 and 0.5, respectively. For each query, the top 10 documents are considered. Weight decay parameter (λ) is set to 10[-5] to avoid model overfitting. After every 5 iterations we check the model's validation performance and if it is less than the current validation click perplexity, we update it. The entire coding is done in Pytorch using Google Colab notebook, and all the deep models are trained on the Nvidia K80 GPU.

## **4 CONCLUSIONS** 

In this paper, we propose a novel vertical-aware transformer click model (v-TCM) in which we additionally incorporate vertical information as an input to the transformer encoder, apart from the query and search engine results. Experiments are conducted on a public web log session dataset. We find that: 1) transformer model is performing better in terms of perplexity i.e. click prediction, and NDCG@10 i.e. relevance estimation, as compared to the baseline models. 2) incorporating the vertical as an additional input proves fruitful for our cause since it significantly improves the performance, as compared to the baseline models like CSM and NCM that do not take the verticals into account as per their model architecture. More user behavior information must be exploited in the future for improved session and click modeling, like incorporating the vision bias and mouse movement as additional input features in the proposed click model.
