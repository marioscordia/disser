## **2. Related Works** 

HAR based on computer vision techniques involves analyzing video or image data to recognize and classify human activities. Here are some common computer vision techniques used in HAR: object detection, pose estimation, motion analysis, feature extraction, action recognition models, temporal modeling, and fusion techniques. So, in order to develop applications for HAR, several research works and studies used deep learning and computer vision techniques. 

Te authors of [11] described a method for detecting human activities in video sequences using local space–time characteristics and SVMs. Te method detects localized motion events that are resistant to fuctuations in scale, frequency, and velocity and then utilizes these properties to identify six human movements (walking, jogging, running, boxing, hand waving, and hand clapping) from a dataset of 2391 sequences. Combining these characteristics with SVM classifers results in excellent recognition accuracy across several contexts, exceeding previous approaches. 

An action detection framework was proposed by Saha et al. [12] for the purpose of classifying and identifying the start and fnish locations of actions in a video series. Two streams are used for frame-level detection and classifcation: RGB for appearance and optical fow for motion. Based on

## **3. TheoreticalStudy:AdverseEffectsofDrugson Body, Brain, and Behaviors of Addicted Students** 

Addiction is a chronic condition defned by obsessive substance seeking and use, notwithstanding the negative efects. Clinically, this is known as substance use disorder (SUD). Addiction theories, such as the illness model of addiction and behavioral psychology, argue that addiction is caused by complex neurological, psychological, and environmental processes rather than human decisions or moral failure [1]. According to the disease model, addiction changes brain function, notably in the brain’s reward system (part of the limbic system), which is dominated by dopamine. Tis system regulates pleasure and reward, and drugs stimulate it by releasing excessive dopamine, resulting in extreme sensations of euphoria. Over time, the brain’s chemistry adjusts, resulting in a dependence in which people require drugs to feel normal rather than to enjoy pleasure. From the perspective of behavioral psychology, the operant conditioning model explains addiction through reinforcement mechanisms. Drug use is positively reinforced by the immediate pleasurable advantages (the “high”), but negative reinforcement occurs when drug use alleviates withdrawal symptoms or mental distress. Tese methods may persuade people, particularly vulnerable teenagers, to continue taking drugs despite the negative consequences. Understanding the scientifc origins of addiction explains why the following detrimental efects occur in student populations: 

- Adverse efects of addiction student bodies [1, 20]: 

- A compromised immune system, which increases the risk of disease and infection. 

- Heart issues induced by injectable drugs include abnormal heart rhythms, heart attacks, collapsed veins, and blood vessel infections. 

- Nausea and stomach pain, which might lead to appetite changes and weight loss. 

- Seizures, strokes, mental confusion, and brain injury are all risks. 

- Respiratory disease. 

- Memory, attention to detail, and decision-making challenges make daily living more challenging. 

- One of the most devastating health consequences of drug abuse is death. Deaths from synthetic opioids and heroin have increased the most. In the previous year, 212,000 persons aged 12 and up had frst consumed heroin. Every day, nearly 90 Americans are killed by an opioid overdose [21]. 

- Adverse efects of addiction on student brains [1, 20]: 

- Cocaine, nicotine, alcohol, marijuana, and other types of drugs have an infuence on the brain’s “reward” circuit, which is part of the limbic system. Tis area of the brain has an impact on instinct and emotion. Drugs disrupt this system by fooding the brain with enormous amounts of dopamine, a brain chemical that assists in the control of emotions and pleasurable feelings. Tis dopamine spike is what causes the “high.” It is one of the leading causes of drug addiction. 

- Drug use may appear to be voluntary at frst, and it can alter brain chemistry. Tis can change the way the brain works and damage a person’s ability to make decisions. It has the potential to induce intense cravings and compulsive drug use. Over time, this tendency might lead to substance dependency or drug and alcohol addiction. 

- Alcohol afects the brain in both the short and long terms, as well as disrupting communication networks. Tese can infuence mood, behavior, and other cognitive functions. 

- Alcohol-induced dietary defciencies, seizures, and liver disease can all result in brain damage. According to research, alcohol-related brain problems may frequently be treated with appropriate therapy. 

- Adverse efects of addiction on student behaviors: 

- Addiction may seriously afect a student’s conduct, afecting both their personal and academic lives. Addicts at school, whether they be from drugs, alcohol, or other substances frequently sufer from poor judgment, impulsivity, and lack of self-control. ## **4. Methodology** 

Te proposed evaluation framework, as well as the steps of the proposed study technique, is depicted in Figure 1. Tese steps are as follows: 

- Video Segmentation: Te input videos are frst divided into individual frames using a video segmentation algorithm. Tis guarantees the temporal continuity of student activity while allowing for the analysis of each frame separately. 

- Handcrafted MediaPipe API Feature Extraction: Using the MediaPipe API, we are able to extract information on each frame’s skeletal joints, including 33 important skeletal joints like the elbows, knees, and shoulders. Tese joints provide the basis for handcrafted information such as joint locations (66 features, X and Y coordinates), Euclidean distances between subsequent joints (33 features), and joint movement velocity across frames (33 features). Tese handcrafted features ofer precise spatial and temporal data on bodily movement, which is essential for recognizing abnormal behaviors. In addition, these features focusing on specifc movements that correlate with stress, anxiety, and aggression. Tese movements are crucial indicators of abnormal 

behavior in addicted students, and the feature extraction process ensures accurate modeling of these signs. 

- Deep Feature Extraction using Pretrained Models: In parallel, we used pretrained deep learning models (YOLOv7 for body identifcation, MobileNetV2, ResNet100, Xception, VGG19, and InceptionResnetV2 for deep feature extraction). Tese models were used to extract high-level features from cropped frames, capturing abstract patterns of both normal and abnormal student behavior. Tese deep features complement the handcrafted features by providing a more detailed knowledge of behavior, especially in complex scenarios, and enhance the model’s ability to distinguish between normal and abnormal behaviors. 

- Feature Fusion: We then integrated the handcrafted features (skeletal joint information) with the deep features obtained by the pretrained models. Tis fusion enables us to capture both domain-specifc and abstract behavioral representations, increasing the model’s resilience in detecting abnormal behaviors. 

- Behavioral Classifcation Model: Te fused features are fed via a deep learning architecture that includes two TimeDistributed layers, one BiLSTM layer (for temporal dependencies), and numerous Dense layers with ReLu activation function and Dense layer with sigmoid activation function for classifcation purposes. Tis design guarantees that both spatial and temporal patterns in the student’s behavior are accurately captured and evaluated as normal (Class: 0) or abnormal (Class: 1). In this step, we used cross-validation technique with _k_ 5, in which the data were divided into fvefolds, with each fold being used once for validation and the remaining four serving as training data. Tis technique was used to ensure that each fold maintains the original dataset’s distribution of the target classes, addressing class imbalance concerns.

## **. Conclusions and Future Works** 

In this study, we introduced a novel method aimed at identifying abnormal behaviors among addicted students in school settings. Our approach focused on developing a predictive model to create an intelligent detector for such behaviors, addressing an increasingly crucial need in educational environments. Tis system not only improves the identifcation of addiction-related behaviors but also prepares the path for future integration into educational systems, which might provide a preventive approach to drug misuse. Aside from the technological contributions, this study emphasizes the necessity of early detection techniques in ensuring students’ academic and personal growth. Te suggested approach, by notifying educational professionals and guardians, might play an important role in reducing the spread of addiction and its accompanying consequences. 

Our empirical validation, using the “Real-Life Violence Situations Dataset,” confrmed the system’s ability to recognize abnormal behavior. Handcrafted features from skeletal joint recognition using the MediaPipe API were combined with classifers including KNN, RF, SVM, and NB. Te accuracy of these classifers ranged from 56.6% for NB to 89.7% for SVM, demonstrating the system’s ability to successfully use spatial features for behavior recognition. 

In addition to handcrafted features, we used transfer learning models to extract deep features from CNN architectures such as VGG19, Xception, ResNet100, and InceptionResnetV2. Tese features were processed using TimeDistributed, LSTM, and Dense layers, resulting in accuracy ranges of 79.56% to 93.33%, with MobileNetV2 surpassing the others. Te combination of handcrafted and deep features resulted in a fnal model architecture with an accuracy of 97.5%, demonstrating the strength of integrating spatial and temporal data for abnormal behavior classifcation. 

While the current study establishes a solid framework for recognizing abnormal student behavior, some limitations must be addressed. First, the model is primarily reliant on publicly accessible dataset, such as the “Real-Life Violence Situations Dataset,” which may not adequately refect the range of behaviors unique to school environments. As a result, future research will focus on creating a specifc dataset suited to addicted students in real-world educational environments. Tis dataset will comprise not just behavioral 

actions, but also multimodal inputs such as facial expressions, voice analysis, and physiological indications such as heart rate. Furthermore, environmental circumstances such as camera positioning, illumination, and resolution can all have an infuence on the model’s present accuracy, afecting real-time detection performance. Future enhancements will focus on fne-tuning the system so that it can perform well in a variety of educational settings, including low-resource contexts. Finally, more sophisticated strategies, such as model assembly and the incorporation of other data modalities, will be investigated to improve the model’s resilience and generalizability. We also want to include user input from educational professionals to continually increase the system’s sensitivity to specifc behaviors associated with addiction and other mental health concerns.
