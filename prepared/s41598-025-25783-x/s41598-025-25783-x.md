## **OPEN A hybrid model integrating RoBERTa, TF-IDF, and attention mechanism for medical query intent classification** 

**Zejian Liang[1] , Yunxiang Zhao[1]**[] **, Haiwen Xu[1] , Hong Huang[2] & Luning Chen[3]** 

**In medical retrieval scenarios, accurate identification of user query intent is pivotal for enhancing retrieval relevance and knowledge augmentation effectiveness. However, existing approaches exhibit polarized performance: traditional shallow models (e.g., TF-IDF, Bag-of-Words) struggle to capture contextual semantics, while pre-trained language models, despite their strong deep semantic representation capabilities, often overlook keyword weighting and category-specific priors. This leads to suboptimal accuracy for long-tail categories and susceptibility to confusion from emotional or colloquial query formulations. To address these challenges, this study proposes a hybrid model integrating RoBERTa-wwm-ext with sentence-level TF-IDF and category-centroid TF-IDF via a three-branch attention fusion mechanism. The model employs adaptive attention mechanisms to dynamically weight deep-shallow features at the sample level, while incorporating category-centroid vectors as explicit semantic anchors to mitigate class imbalance issues. Experimental evaluations on the KUAKE-QIC dataset demonstrate that our approach achieves an accuracy of 0.824 and a Macro-F1 score of 0.800, outperforming all baseline models by 2.3% points. These results highlight the synergistic benefits of deep-shallow feature interaction, ensuring robust recall for dominant categories while significantly improving precision for minority classes. This work provides a generalizable framework for medical intent classification that balances semantic integrity with keyword-driven priors, laying a more reliable foundation for applications such as clinical question-answering systems, medical search ranking, and intelligent triage systems.** 

**Keywords** Medial intent classification, RoBERTa, TF-IDF, Attention mechanism, Knowledge-enhanced retrieval

## **Related work** 

The evolution of medical text classification has witnessed a paradigm shift from traditional methods to deep learning techniques. Early approaches primarily relied on rule-based systems or classical machine learning algorithms. Vector space models, leveraging term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) and bagof-words (BoW) representations, were initially employed to capture lexical significance[8] . Subsequently, word embedding technologies such as Word2Vec[9] and GloVe[10] emerged, enabling semantic relationships between terms to be modeled through continuous vector spaces. Faris et al.[11] demonstrated the utility of particle swarm optimization for feature selection combined with a one-vs-rest SVM ensemble, achieving patient department classification. While these traditional methods performed adequately on small-scale datasets, they heavily depended on expert-driven feature engineering and struggled to capture deep contextual semantics. In recent years, deep learning has revolutionized medical text processing. Architectures such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs)/long short-term memory (LSTM) networks have enabled automatic feature learning from raw data, reducing reliance on manual design[3] . For instance, Du et al.[12] proposed the ML-Net model, which employs deep neural networks for multi-label classification of biomedical literature, significantly enhancing efficiency over traditional approaches. Chen et al.[13] integrated LSTM networks into medical text classification frameworks, improving the extraction of critical symptom keywords and descriptive elements from large-scale medical corpora. Zhang et al.[14] explored CNN architectures for text classification, demonstrating how convolutional operations and max-pooling layers capture local textual features to achieve efficient categorization. Liu et al.[15] introduced a recursive neural network (RNN) framework combining online language understanding and language modeling, showcasing superior performance on context-dependent tasks. The advent of attention mechanisms, pioneered by Bahdanau et al.[16] , further advanced model capabilities, yielding improved performance in machine translation and text classification. Chai et al.[17] leveraged statistical text classification to identify medical IT incidents, achieving notable accuracy gains. Ali et al.[18] and Ozyegen et al.[19] separately proposed machine learning-based and word-level text highlighting approaches for medical text classification, enhancing clinical data analysis efficiency. More recently, Liang et al.[20] introduced a medical text classification framework combining ZEN and capsule networks, while Luo et al.[21] demonstrated the efficacy of the bilingual Taiyi model for diverse biomedical tasks. These advancements collectively underscore the transformative potential of deep learning and hybrid architectures in addressing the unique challenges of medical text processing. 

This progress has catalyzed the development of domain-adapted BERT variants, including BioBERT[24] for biomedical texts, and Chinese-specific models such as MC-BERT[25] , BERT-Healthcare[26] , and ClinicalBERT[27] . Innovative hybrid architectures further extend these capabilities: Li et al.[28] proposed ECC-BERT, while Wa et al.[29] and Yu et al.[30] introduced BERT-CNN and TinyBERT-CNN fusion models, respectively, combining BERT’s semantic robustness with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance classification performance. Additionally, Wang et al.[31] demonstrated the efficacy of discriminative pre-training and prompt tuning techniques, and Peng et al.[32] showcased transfer learning approaches for medical text classification. Zhang et al.[33] highlighted BERT’s applicability in biological sequence analysis, achieving notable results in predicting lncRNA subcellular localization. By incorporating large-scale medical literature and clinical note corpora during pre-training, these models demonstrate superior adaptation to medical terminology and discourse patterns.

To bridge this gap, our work proposes a three-branch hybrid architecture combining contextual embeddings (RoBERTa-wwm-ext), local keyword salience (sentence-level TF-IDF), and global category priors (category-centroid TF-IDF). This design aims to achieve both semantic precision and category-level robustness for ultra-short medical queries. 

## **Methodology** 

## **Model framework** 

This paper proposes a multi-feature fusion hybrid model, whose architecture is structured into three hierarchical components from top to bottom as shown in Fig. 1: the Feature Generation Layer, responsible for extracting semantic information and lexical features from medical queries; the Feature Fusion Layer, which integrates crossmodal information through adaptive weighting to dynamically balance semantic and lexical representations; and the Classification Decision Layer, performing final intent classification using fused features for discriminative multi-class prediction. 

The first component of the model is feature generation, whose core objective is to construct complementary information representations for the same query. The RoBERTa branch contributes long-range contextualized deep semantic vectors, the sentence-level TF-IDF branch highlights the most discriminative medical keywords within the current sentence for intent discrimination, while the category-centric TF-IDF branch incorporates global category priors derived from training corpus statistics. This integration effectively mitigates data sparsity issues arising from long-tail categories and rare terminology by encoding these priors into the representation space. All three feature streams are linearly projected to identical dimensions, establishing a unified dimensionality basis for subsequent weighted fusion operations, as detailed in the Feature Generation Layer of Table 1. 

The second component of the model is attentive fusion, which dynamically determines the contribution ratio of the three feature branches based on query-specific content. Through learnable nonlinear scoring functions and softmax normalization, the model automatically amplifies the feature branch that best explains the current query intent. When queries contain highly indicative medical terminology, the TF-IDF branch receives greater emphasis. For semantic dependencies requiring long-range contextual understanding, the RoBERTa branch becomes more dominant. In scenarios with extreme class imbalance, the category centroid branch provides supplementary support for underrepresented classes. The resultant fused representation integrates contextual semantics, keyword salience, and categorical knowledge, demonstrating enhanced robustness against noise and data distribution skew. 

Inter-branch attention vs. intra-sequence self-attention. RoBERTa’s self-attention operates within a sequence to model token–token dependencies and form contextual representations. Our fusion module instead attends across branches over three heterogeneous features (RoBERTa embedding, sentence-level TF-IDF, categorycentroid TF-IDF). It acts as a sample-wise gating mechanism to adaptively weight each branch for a specific query, rather than recomputing token-level dependencies as in Transformer self-attention. ## **RoBERTa-based semantic encoder** 

RoBERTa is a pre training language model based on Transformer architecture. Transformer is a neural network architecture completely based on self attention mechanism, including encoder and decoder. 

the Transformer architecture is alternately stacked by multi head self attention and feedforward networks. BERT and RoBERta are variants of its Encoder. Each layer first executes Multi-Head Self-Attention extract intra sentence global dependencies, and then provides nonlinear mapping through Add&Norm and Feed Forward. Residual connection and layer normalization jointly stabilize the deep gradient. Compared with RNN, Transformer has the advantages of parallel computing and long-distance modeling. Compared with CNN, the attention mechanism can dynamically focus on any position, eliminating the limitation of fixed receptive field. 

RoBERTa introduces three key enhancements over the original BERT training paradigm: (1) Removal of Next-Sentence Prediction to eliminate task-related noise. (2) Adoption of dynamic masking, where 15% of tokens are randomly re-masked in each training epoch. (3) Utilization of larger mini-batch sizes and extended training steps. These optimizations significantly improve representation quality and transfer robustness. For Chinese applications, the HFL team from Harbin Institute of Technology conducted continued pre-training on 160 GB of Chinese encyclopedic, news, Q&A, and forum data, developing RoBERTa-wwm-ext. Its critical innovation lies in Whole-Word Masking, which uniformly masks all subword components of Chinese terms (e.g., treating “甲状腺功能减退” as a complete medical compound rather than fragmented “甲状 + 腺 + 功 能…”). ## **Experiments** 

## **Problem definition and dataset description** 

In medical triage, accurate intent classification of patient queries significantly enhances operational efficiency. However, the profound complexity of medical domain knowledge and the inherent ambiguity/diversity of user expressions pose substantial challenges for precise categorization. Medical inquiries span a broad spectrum of scenarios, including disease diagnosis, etiology analysis, therapeutic regimens, and hospital guidance, necessitating classification models with both strong generalization capacity and fine-grained discriminative power. This study addresses these challenges within the context of mobile medical search, where queries are typically extremely short, use colloquial terminology, and exhibit severe class imbalance. The model must jointly capture contextual semantics and identify critical medical terminology to achieve high-precision multi-class intent determination. The experiments are conducted on the KUAKE-QIC dataset, sourced from the Chinese Biomedical Language Understanding Evaluation (CBLUE) benchmark maintained by Alibaba Cloud’s Tianchi platform (aliyun.com). The dataset comprises 6,931 training samples and 1,955 validation samples, annotated across 11 intent categories: diagnosis (As detailed in Table 4), cause analysis (cause), therapeutic methods (therapy), hospitalization guidance (advice), laboratory metric interpretation (metric), disease description (diseaseDesc), prognostic outcomes (outcome), clinical precautions (caution), therapeutic efficacy (effect), medical costs (cost), and an other category. As detailed in Table 5 training set class distribution, therapeutic methods and “other” categories dominate the dataset, while critical classes such as cause analysis and metric interpretation are severely underrepresented. This task thus confronts dual challenges: class imbalance and sparse contextual information in ultra-short text queries.

## **Discussion** 

This work examined a hybrid architecture that fuses deep contextual representations (RoBERTa-wwm-ext) with sentence-level TF-IDF and class-centroid TF-IDF via attention. The comparative study (Table 9) shows a clear advantage over traditional shallow models and single-branch deep encoders, with the proposed model attaining Accuracy = 0.824 and Macro-F1 = 0.800 on KUAKE-QIC. The ablation analysis (Table 11) indicates that both lexical branches contribute complementary gains (> 1 pp each in Macro-F1) and that adaptive attention is superior to naïve concatenation (+ 1.4 pp), underscoring the necessity of (i) explicit lexical priors and (ii) samplewise feature re-weighting. Hyperparameter sensitivity (Table 12) further suggests that performance is most responsive to max input length and TF-IDF vocabulary size; with ultra-short medical queries, max_len = 256 and tfidf_max_features = 200 strike the best balance between semantic coverage and sparsity control, while larger vocabularies (≥ 400) introduce noise that depresses Macro-F1. Among attention variants, dot-product and multi-head (4) yield the strongest Macro-F1 (0.799) with comparable training time, supporting dot attention as a practical default. 

At the label level (Table 13; Fig. 3), the model delivers near-ceiling performance on head or keywordsalient classes (e.g., Therapy, Diagnosis, Cost), confirming that deep semantics and keyword salience are jointly captured. The residual errors concentrate in semantically diffuse or affect-laden categories (e.g., Outcome, Other), where boundaries overlap with DiseaseDesc and sentiment-like intents. We also observe “long-tail migration” in the confusion matrix: Other disperses widely, acting as a sink for open-set queries; nevertheless, minority classes (Effect, Cause, Metric) benefit from the centroid prior and sentence-level TF-IDF, maintaining competitive F1 despite limited support. Based on the above analysis, further improvements can be made: (1) label space refinement (sub-typing Other), (2) knowledge-augmented features (medical ontologies, synonym maps) to reduce ambiguity, and (3) confidence-based rejection/handoff to human triage to mitigate high-risk misclassifications. 

Generalisability and deployment. The method targets ultra-short Chinese medical queries with class imbalance. While the fusion design is model-agnostic, transferability to other domains/languages may depend on domain lexicons and centroid stability. For real-world use, the footprint remains moderate (single RoBERTa encoder + two lightweight projections + small MLP), and attention adds minimal latency; measured training/ inference times in Table 12 suggest feasibility for online intent routing. 

Limitations and future directions. (1) Single-dataset evaluation: results may overestimate in-domain performance; cross-dataset validation (e.g., other CBLUE tasks or real-world logs) is a priority. (2) Open-set and ambiguity: Other and Outcome remain challenging; hierarchical tagging and ontology-aware disambiguation are promising. (3) LLM comparison: when LLMs are available, few-shot prompting can be competitive; however, our hybrid remains attractive for efficiency, stability, and controllability. Future work will integrate structured medical knowledge (UMLS/CHIP ontologies), explore sequence-aware variants for dialogic contexts, and incorporate confidence calibration with selective prediction. 

## **Conclusion** 

On the KUAKE-QIC benchmark, the proposed RoBERTa-wwm-ext + sentence TF-IDF + category-centric TFIDF framework with attentive fusion delivers Macro-F1 = 0.800 and Accuracy = 0.824, validating the effectiveness of deep–shallow complementarity and adaptive weighting. 

- (1) Compared with the RoBERTa-only baseline, our model improves Macro-F1 by 2.3% points and Accuracy by 1.3% points, without substantial complexity increase. 

- (2) Ablation studies show that each TF-IDF branch contributes ≈ 1 pp Macro-F1 individually, and attentive fusion provides an additional ≈ 1.4 pp over static concatenation—significantly boosting macro-precision without sacrificing recall, especially for long-tail categories. 

- (3) Category-level analysis and the confusion matrix indicate strong performance on structurally coherent, keyword-rich classes, while TF-IDF centroid priors help rare classes maintain high F1, confirming the generalization of category-specific representations under class imbalance. 

Overall, the approach is accurate, parameter-efficient, and deployment-friendly, providing a practical recipe for intent classification in ultra-short medical queries.
