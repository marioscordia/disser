## **Abstract** 

The identification of abnormal behavior has several applications. There are several ways, ranging from classical to deep learning based. It may be used to monitor campuses, banks, transportation, and airports. In many circumstances, the context determines whether real-life events are common or unusual. Recent video surveillance anomaly detection systems are good enough, but they come at a significant computational cost and require particular hardware resources. When it comes to real-time anomaly detection, extra emphasis must be paid on lowering model complexity, which causes computational and memory demands. This study attempts to find real-world abnormalities in CCTV recordings, such as violence, detention, property destruction, assault, burglary, blast and theft. The impact of these abnormalities on public safety is enormous. The study also provides a low-cost algorithm for detecting crowd irregularities. The proposed model uses the convolutional neural network based on DenseNet121 as the feature extractor. Our suggested framework has an AUC of 86.63 percent on the UCF-Crime dataset and obtains new stateof-the-art performance

## **1. Introduction** 

Surveillance recordings are becoming more common in a range of environments to monitor human activities and prevent crime. It can be helpful if someone is viewing the footage and triggering an alarm if something out of the ordinary occurs. However, these occurrences are rare, and most of the time, the individual watching the footage will not notice anything unusual. These unusual events might be looked on as anomalies since they exhibit patterns that do not match what is regarded as normal [1]. Recent research, however, suggests that anomaly detection, in particular, autonomous human behavior analysis, is still an open issue [2]. Researchers have been attempting to develop strong anomaly detection algorithms capable of automating the process of monitoring and detecting unexpected occurrences in surveillance recordings. The main difficulties that lie in the way of anomaly identification in autonomous video monitoring are discussed as. First, the definition of an anomaly varies depending on the context. **2. Related Work** 

## _2.1.  Traditional Methods: Considers low level features._ 

The early research focused on trajectory-based anomaly. Calderara et al. [7] proposed the two methods the first being the capacity to use several video cameras to follow and identify single person trajectories in a busy space, and the second is a new class of novelty detection techniques based on graph spectral analysis. Jiang et al. [8] used the method which involved evaluating video occurrences at many levels, taking into account both geographical and temporal context. Tung et al. [9] described that in three ways, the suggested technique extends the goal-based framework. With sparse reconstruction analysis, Li et al. [10] offer a novel trajectory-based visual abnormal behavior detection technique. However, because the technique strongly relies on continually monitoring the item of interest, one key problem is occlusion. Because of these disadvantages, low-level characteristics were emphasized for feature extraction instead. Zhang et al. [11] introduced a new anomaly detection framework that combines motion and appearance signals to recognize unusual objects and behaviors in video and used support vector data description. Wang et al. [12] proposed the OCELM model. These conventional procedures, despite their shown efficacy on benchmark datasets, are still ineffective when applied to a different domain. Additionally, they struggle to adjust to irregularities they have never encountered. Due to these factors, the majority of recent research has been on using Deep Neural Networks to detect anomalies. There is no longer a need for manual feature development because these neural networks produce useful and differentiating traits on their own.

## _3.2. Dataset Details_ 

**UCF-Crime Dataset** : Sultani et al. [6] constructed the UCF-Crime Dataset since the prior datasets were rather modest in size (2018). Accidents, burglary, explosion, fighting, robbery, shooting, theft, shoplifting, and vandalism are among the 13 real world abnormalities in this dataset. These text inquiries were not confined to English; they were also conducted in other languages (using Google Translate). 1,900 videos total, including 950 uncut real-world surveillance recordings and 950 regular recordings, are included in the collection. The complete file has a resolution of 240x320 pixels and around 128 hours of data. 1900 films totalling 128 hours make up the UCF crime dataset. The dataset is useful for the problem of video categorization since the length of a video is on average 4 minutes, with 70% of videos being under 3 minutes. ## _3.3. DenseNet Model_ 

ResNet [20] inspired DenseNet [21]. Dense connections, or the unique layer connectivity of DenseNet, play a significant role in giving specific benefits. The main advantages of such deep networks over ResNet [20] and Inception Net [22] include improved model comprehension, reduced complexity, fewer parameter requirements, lower computing and power consumption, and increased accuracy. The input data or gradient that was present in the majority of the levels by the time the last layer was reached vanishes as the network gets deeper. By directly tying together all the layers with similar feature sizes, DenseNet circumvents the gradient vanishing issue. Deeper within the network, more general features may be extracted, which is the main goal of employing DenseNet architecture as a feature extractor. The feature extraction procedure has been carried out using the 121-layer, pre-trained Densely Connected Convolutional Neural Network (DenseNet-121). The form of this model that we employed in our investigation was trained using the substantial ImageNet dataset, which is freely accessible and was developed by Huang et al. [21]. The DenseNet121 design consists of 4 dense blocks, 3 transition layers, and a convolution and pooling layer at the beginning. The categorization layer is the last layer present after these layers. In our DenseNet implementation, we employed categorical cross entropy as a loss function. The model is optimised using the stochastic gradient descent approach, and the cost function for calculating the reconstruction loss is mean squared error. In order to avoid the model from being overfit, we adopted an early ending regularisation technique, which causes training to end when the loss stops improving.

## **5. Conclusion** 

This study proposes a method that can identify anomalous behavior in almost real-time. High Performance Computing through the usage of NVIDIA GPUs with creative solutions that may be used to monitor campuses and airports is one of the applications' advantages. The experimental findings show that the suggested technique successfully detects abnormal behavior in a promising manner that is almost as accurate as actual behavior. This research offered a unique computer vision method for detecting anomalous behavior such as robbery, assault, and other forms of criminal activity that can constitute a harm to the community. For the framework, we used a DenseNet like cascade of deep convolutional neural networks. After training a lightweight yet robust DenseNet, our system attained an overall accuracy of 86.63 percent. To increase the accuracy of the system, more supporting research is needed to eliminate complications such shadows and non-leaving lookalikes engaged in abnormal behavior
